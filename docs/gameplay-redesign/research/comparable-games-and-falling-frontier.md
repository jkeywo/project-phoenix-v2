The strongest references give officers different operating problems that affect each other: incomplete observations, vulnerable operations, competing resource demands and consequences that persist beyond an exchange of fire. A different setting helps us see these patterns without inheriting a conventional bridge roster.

This is a desk research pass using developer/publisher descriptions, official project repositories and manuals. It is **not a hands-on comparison or evidence of enjoyable workload over time**. The evidence column records what a source actually supports; the transfer and risk columns are Phoenix design inferences. Public descriptions can omit limitations. Advertised or developing designs are identified explicitly. Artemis SBS/Cosmos and Thorium/Thorium Nova are treated as reference families, rather than assuming every version has identical features. X4 and Starsector appeared in more than one source-session category and are counted once. Barotrauma is included with submarine references because of its activity structure, although its setting is Europa.

## Falling Frontier: deeper examination

Falling Frontier is the additional reference requested by the user. Its Steam page currently says **Coming soon**. The publisher's wiki explicitly says its material is subject to change. These are **advertised design mechanics**, not verified released behaviour or demonstrated player experience. [Developer store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1280190/Falling_Frontier/), [publisher intelligence overview](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Falling_Frontier/Intelligence_%26_Espionage).

### Information is an operation

The publisher describes passive observation of signatures, active sensing that can reveal the observer, broad short-range coverage versus a longer directional search, and celestial bodies blocking sensor line of sight. Reorienting a reconnaissance station takes time and interrupts its operation. Captured personnel can reveal different information according to their position and knowledge; the description does not promise a general spy/sabotage system. [Intelligence and espionage](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Falling_Frontier/Intelligence_%26_Espionage).

**Phoenix inference:** Sensors should choose which uncertainty is worth resolving, not merely select every unidentified return. A directional suite (S098) can keep a stable search while its operator listens, then require an active decision to redirect it. Passive receivers (S097) can preserve concealment while producing incomplete evidence. G105–G106 distinguish a plausible contact from information sufficient to act. Helm chooses a route that changes coverage; Comms checks a claimed destination or need; weapons decide whether the evidence warrants commitment. No new sensor should require a separate permanent seat by itself.

### Resources have a location and a journey

Spaceports, refineries, depots and service outposts have different roles. Fuel and materiel must be delivered; transports can be attacked and their cargo lost. Routing can be automated, while service availability still depends on actual resources at the destination. [Logistics](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Falling_Frontier/Logistics).

**Phoenix inference:** A few tracked deliveries are enough to connect Engineering's repair estimate to Comms' promise, Navigation's route and defence's protection priority (G108). An automated supply System should fulfil a chosen order and report a blocked route. The crew's decision is whether to escort, reroute, transfer from its own reserve, repair locally or abandon an obligation. Time spent producing anonymous supplies is not inherently an interesting player job. S041–S042, S084, S089 and S092 already cover the hardware; the new insight belongs in the delivery rules.

### Loss produces new decisions

The advertised combat includes subsystem damage, collateral from misses or deflections, and smaller ships exploiting hiding places. Officers have traits and skills; injured or lost crew affect capability, and escape pods permit recovery or capture. Rescue can expose a ship to an ambush. [Developer description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1280190/Falling_Frontier/), [crew and admirals](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Falling_Frontier/Crew_and_Admirals).

**Phoenix inference:** A defeated contact can become a rescue, evidence or security problem rather than simply vanish. S100 covers locating and recovering pods; G064/G071 connect recovery to continued capability, while G109 tracks which distress claims have actually been checked. Keep the rescue morally and tactically legible: universal fake distress would teach players to ignore rescues. Captured information should have source, confidence and relevance. Injuries affect simulated personnel; they must not eject a human player from useful play.

### A bounded test situation — proposal

A service outpost has promised a repair, but its material shipment is late. Passive sensing finds intermittent emissions near the convoy's last report. Active scanning could resolve the contact while revealing the crew's approach. A damaged transport requests help, then a pod beacon appears beyond an occluding body. The crew can protect the convoy, investigate the beacon, supply the outpost itself or negotiate a different repair destination.

This is an **abstract prototype**, not an edit to Falling Skyway or any authored Phoenix scenario. Start with a small set of actors and ordinary physical rules. Engineering maintains reserves and estimates restoration; Sensors tests competing explanations; Comms checks reports and revised promises; Helm selects a safe observation/operation geometry; weapons/defence protect whichever operation the crew commits to. A sixth work bundle remains to be selected and tested rather than manufactured through an extra confirmation step. Run with different whole-System assistants and require useful work for 2, 6 and 8 humans before expanding it.

For each activity prototype, record quiet-time engagement, meaningful event decisions per Station, longest unoccupied stretch, simultaneous urgent demands, recovery after interruption, communication that changes a choice, and who actually decides. Review overload, waiting, repeated maintenance and domination separately. A busy bridge is not enough evidence of flow. Do not adopt a mechanic merely because it fits space fiction, appears in several games or can generate more alerts.

Lower-fit references remain in the inventory to show where ideas were considered. Free construction, on-foot activity, MMO economies, empire command, betrayal and hunger/sleep routines have identifiable relevance but do not become implied requirements. The next proposal pass should select a coherent activity set and a few variants of it; it should not implement all 35 references.
