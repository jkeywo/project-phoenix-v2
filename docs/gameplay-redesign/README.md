# Phoenix gameplay redesign GDD

Start with the [current GDD](../gdd/index.md), [current implementation baseline](../gdd/foundation/current-baseline.md), [existing Plan baseline](../gdd/future/roadmap-baseline.md), [research sources](./research/README.md), [ship systems catalogue](./ship-systems.md) and [game systems catalogue](./game-systems.md). These are the comparison inputs for this proposal.

The [comparable-games research](./research/comparable-games-and-falling-frontier.md) expands these inputs with all references from **Find comparable space games**, including non-space games, and a deeper examination of Falling Frontier. Its proposed transfers are incorporated into both catalogues; its integration table identifies candidates to test before selecting the gameplay model.

Phoenix should give each player an interesting operating problem, worthwhile interventions and useful reasons to talk to the crew. This proposal starts from those activities before selecting a Station roster, fictional technology or combat model. It evaluates what the current game and roadmap contribute, what should change, and which research ideas could improve the experience.

The catalogues are broad inventories. Their presence here does not commit Phoenix to every system, reinstate historical research exclusions or replace the delivery roadmap. The existing GDD remains the comparison baseline. This draft introduces no gameplay implementation, network redesign or new setting canon.

The [feature reconciliation report](./architecture/feature-reconciliation.html) proposes twelve shared mechanism families and ten operable ship-System families, maps world/supporting features, and accounts for all 216 catalogue entries. Review these groupings before developing individual System details. The [architecture synthesis brief](./architecture/README.md) records the scope: consolidate selected gameplay into reusable mechanisms, authored Entity/Station/Console/System composition, explicit local and remote data flows, and visual Workshop authoring. The [HTML architecture report](./architecture/index.html) begins with ownership and data-flow diagrams; further sections remain in development. **AD01 selects one controlling Console per operable System**, with that Console assigned to a Station through fixed or human-seeking placement. Information sharing does not confer control. Existing networking and individual System AI remain constraints.

The physical composition is separate: a ship has multiple decks with 2D compartment layouts, and each System's equipment belongs to one compartment. Workshop templates can be made from components, Systems or compartments. **AD39–52 select planar penetration on a weighted random deck, independently of shot Z height.** Material properties and HP belong to compartments; resistance is charged by distance through them, and individual damage events apply component status effects through authored rules. See the [damage flow](./architecture/index.html#damage-decks). As compartment HP falls, its effective resistance progressively falls too; the exact curve remains tunable. At zero HP a condition disables the contents, and compartment restoration removes only that condition. AD50 clears it at a modest authored HP fraction above zero and below full restoration, then reapplies it only if HP reaches zero again.

**AD52 separates physical destruction, disablement and abandonment.** Compartment HP depletion alone does not destroy the ship; a supported catastrophe does. Crew can abandon early, and loss of engines and power can establish disablement. Individual AI uses permitted observations/Comms to recognise that an opponent is out of action, then responds through its ordinary objectives and doctrine rather than waiting for an explosion.

**AD53 selects non-hostile standby during abandonment.** The normal procedure issues cease-fire/standby orders while retaining supported evacuation, life support and distress functions where possible. Ordinary command authority, individual System AI and physical transition limits apply. **AD54 requires human initiation when human players are aboard**; AI may recommend evacuation and execute authorised delegated steps. Fully AI-crewed ships decide through ordinary System AI policies and permitted knowledge.

**AD55 permits return to service during the same scenario.** Authorised crew can return to an abandoned ship through ordinary personnel transfer and restore needed capabilities. The same ship, Systems and Consoles persist with their damage, supplies and other retained state. Abandonment itself imposes no port-recovery requirement; particular repairs still can. Hostile capture remains separate.

**AD56 selects whole-ship operational takeover.** Meeting supported capture requirements transfers authority for the vessel, retaining its physical state and individual System controllers. Located teams can still sabotage equipment and secure areas, but do not acquire partial System control. Behavioural surrender alone does not transfer authority; exact capture requirements remain open. **AD57 grants newly authorised operators access to retained, already-readable Console records**, preserving source, age, uncertainty and Console scope. No universal extraction job is required; ordinary reading time, sharing and ongoing-feed access rules still apply.

**AD58 selects authored compartment connections for internal movement.** Teams and deliveries automatically route between compartments and decks, using shared route queries with activity-specific access, safety and travel rules. Workshop edits the connections alongside the layout. Planning uses permitted knowledge; actual travel can encounter and report unexpected blockages. Damage geometry and resource conduits retain their separate relationships.

**AD59 selects aggregate delivery batches in transit.** Dispatch removes the quantity from source stock; arrival deposits it at the destination. Interruptions preserve the batch's actual location and remaining quantity for supported waiting, rerouting or recovery. Ordinary logistics AI manages routine deliveries using the shared resource, job and route mechanisms, without individual item or porter simulation. **AD60 reserves compatible receiving space at dispatch**, with no reservation for undispatched requests. Normal loading respects the claim, but damage or isolation can still prevent receipt. **AD61 supplies routine delivery capacity through authored logistics throughput and concurrent-work limits**, affected by resources, conditions and routes, without a separate team assigned to each batch.

**AD62 constrains the whole recipe when a required stored output cannot fit**: batch steps wait and continuous conversion slows or pauses, preserving completed work and stock through the blockage. [AD63](./architecture/index.html#recipe-change-disposal) discards unfinished work on a deliberate recipe change, retains unused inputs and completed products where compatible storage permits, and disposes of the excess that cannot be retained. Transfers still use ordinary logistics.

[AD64](./architecture/index.html#mechanism-scheduling) assigns physical processing phases and dependencies to engine code; Workshop composes mechanisms and inspects their dependencies without authoring processor order. Authored AI and job workflow ordering remains separate. [AD65](./architecture/index.html#resource-resolution) selects shared request → resolve → consume accounting, separating resource precedence from processor order while retaining individual System control. [AD66](./architecture/index.html#coupled-resource-reservations) commits each indivisible step's required resources and capacities together, leaving no new partial claims on failure; explicit preparation and real stored supplies persist. [AD67](./architecture/index.html#job-priority-interruption) makes priority changes affect waiting work and subsequent uncommitted stages; interrupting active work requires a separate supported instruction or physical cause.

[AD68](./architecture/index.html#shared-event-catalogue) selects a shared typed event catalogue for eligible job, condition and scenario reactions, with scoped bindings and separate permitted Console observations. [AD69](./architecture/index.html#spatial-fields) shares geometry, source transforms and sampling between environmental fields and suitable equipment emissions, while keeping separate effect rules and real emitter limits. [AD70](./architecture/index.html#field-signal-paths) selects applicable field attenuation along sensor and in-world Comms paths, even between unexposed endpoints, alongside supported local effects. Networking remains intact. [AD71](./architecture/index.html#solid-terrain-signals) adds signal obstruction/attenuation by substantial terrain through authored physical bounds and signal properties. Existing relay equipment can route permitted Comms over viable links around obstacles. [AD72](./architecture/index.html#remote-order-lifetime) selects authored expiry and supersession for undelivered structured orders, with persistent mission orders supported and no automatic cancellation of accepted onboard work. [AD73](./architecture/index.html#remote-order-receipts) selects automatic delivery and accepted/refused receipts over viable in-world links; without a return receipt, the sender retains uncertainty. Completion remains separately reported or observed.

[AD74](./architecture/index.html#transceiver-capacity) rejects message-capacity tracking: no simulated bandwidth pools, traffic budgets, volume congestion or quotas. Physical link/equipment limits, security and knowledge rules remain; pending orders and receipts still handle link outages. [AD75](./architecture/index.html#compartment-boundaries) selects one physical door/hatch state, explicitly shared by movement/access and applicable containment rules. Commanded closure remains distinct from actual sealing; operator knowledge follows normal observation rules. [AD76](./architecture/index.html#sealed-route-access) requires automatic routing to respect deliberate seals and request an authorised access change. **Implementation follows the approved serial roadmap; detailed System contracts are specified when their owning increment is prepared.**

## Agreed design brief

| Requirement | Meaning for the proposal |
|---|---|
| Six players ideal; 2–8 supported | Build for six dedicated human Stations, then prove useful play with 2–8 humans. Ordinary Stations have a dedicated human or AI operator. Do not solve small crews by requiring everyone to combine several manual Stations. The exact roster is still open. |
| Human-seeking exceptions | Some complete consoles may seek an eligible human because their choices are hard to delegate. These must be explicit and included in the receiving player's attention budget. |
| Ongoing demand survives crises | Stable operation should provide reserves and tolerance so an officer can attend to a crisis without routine machinery failing immediately. Ongoing balancing remains relevant. |
| Whole-System assistants | Assistants generally operate complete Systems: auto-fire phasers against an approved target, or operate a tractor near an approved tow target. This is more substantial than an advice popup. |
| Preserve the AI structure | Individual AI per System, communicating through Coordination and operating with limited knowledge. New gameplay must fit this structure; no omniscient ship-wide assistant or central planner replaces it. |
| Optional GM sessions | Support GM-led sessions as a first-class mode alongside autonomous scenarios. A GM can perform actors, shape situations and facilitate play; ordinary crew activity and viable scenarios must not depend on one being present. |
| Different depths in one session | A beginner can retain basic targeting or steering while another player runs deeper manual Systems. Personal complexity choices must coexist on the same ship. |
| Combat and non-combat mix | Rescue, investigation, logistics, diplomacy and crisis response are first-class activity sources alongside fighting. No exact percentage or session length has been fixed. |
| Dynamic problems and information work | World state should give Sensors and Comms changing things to discover, interpret and report, allowing other Stations to react. Emergence is a design direction to investigate, not a claim that procedural generation alone solves workload. |
| Layered combat | Explore interacting detection, engagement, defence, geometry and endurance decisions. Missile → PDC → close broadside is one hypothesis, not a required sequence. |
| Broad catalogue first | Include evidenced current, planned and research systems, with new analysis clearly labelled. Selection comes after comparing activity loops. |
| Guard against overload, waiting, chores and domination | A feature must justify its attention cost, downtime and decision ownership. More buttons, timers or compulsory verbal steps do not inherently improve teamwork. |

## Design interview and decision record

The interview now reviews **each catalogue entry in numeric order: G001–G114 game systems first, then S001–S102 ship systems**. The [ordered System review](./system-review.md) records proposals and review progress. For every entry, review existing/planned Phoenix behaviour and relevant research, propose whether to include it, its extent and form, its interactions, individual complexity, communication and fit with the pillars. Then resolve unanswered decisions one at a time, with concrete options and a recommendation. Explore code/research instead of asking the user to supply facts available there.

Catalogue inclusion is not adoption. An entry can be retained, changed, combined, deferred or rejected. When an entry depends on a later one, record that dependency instead of silently choosing the later design or abandoning the catalogue order. Existing agreed decisions remain constraints; provisional ideas stay marked until their relevant entries are reviewed. Exact Station composition follows evaluation of the activities, even though G001 establishes the rules for Station ownership and work bundles.

### Confirmed constraints

| Decision | Consequence for the redesign |
|---|---|
| Preserve existing networking | Gameplay proposals use the current host authority, Admission and transport. No networking redesign is part of this work. |
| Preserve individual AI per System, communication and limited knowledge | Each assistant acts through its own System authority and available observations/Coordination. AI cannot obtain hidden world truth or another specialist's conclusions merely because the crew needs help. Changes to gameplay may require new authored policies or supported observations, while retaining this structure. |
| GM-led sessions remain supported and always optional | Design for both facilitated and autonomous play. Assess ordinary Station activity without a GM, then assess what actor performance and facilitation add. GM involvement does not grant crew AI extra knowledge or replace the underlying causal game rules. |

The [AI baseline](../gdd/systems/ai-and-backfill.md) describes shared physical rules, information parity and cross-System Coordination. The [existing complexity design](../../pasm/spec/design/console-complexity.yaml) provides authored AI/simplified/detailed rungs: simplified control supplies intent to a System's AI rather than introducing a second actuator writer. These are foundations to preserve, not evidence that every catalogue candidate already has an AI adapter. Communication design must work with human and AI senders/receivers and distinguish routine fact transfer from judgement or a request.

### Confirmed pillar — complementary problem-solving

The primary experience is **solving changing problems together through complementary expertise**, with meaningful individual decisions as a requirement. Specialist mastery and bridge story, including optional GM performance, support that experience.

Reject an activity bundle if one player makes the consequential decisions and the others mainly execute instructions, even if everyone is busy and the ship succeeds. Also reject isolated specialist minigames whose decisions rarely change another operator's options or the crew's outcome.

### Confirmed decision — specialist control and a lightly equipped Captain

The next dependency is how specialist agency survives disagreement with a crew plan. For example, a request for maximum thrust may conflict with Engineering's assessment that it would consume the reserve needed for an imminent rescue.

Engineering has the final decision over power allocation in this conflict. Captain should have very few direct controls. The design must not provide Captain with a duplicate set of specialist controls that turns other players into executors. Leadership can coordinate goals and request support while specialist operators retain the decisions attached to their Systems. The exact small set of Captain controls remains to be selected; no emergency override is adopted by this answer.

### Confirmed decision — Captain is a dedicated coordination seat

Captain is one of the six dedicated reference seats. Missions should require overall coordination. Existing controls such as alert state and selecting AI stances support this role, without giving Captain direct operation of specialist Systems. These controls are examples to retain/evaluate, not the complete final control set.

Mission design must provide a reason to coordinate changing work across Stations. Captain activity must be assessed in combat and non-combat situations, including quiet periods and heavily assisted crews. Do not manufacture the need through mandatory approval of routine actions or a duplicate specialist control panel. The information needed for coordination is the next dependency to resolve.

### Confirmed decision — Captain depends on crew communication

Captain needs the crew to report observations, explain their assessments and discuss what can be done. Talking is a core bridge activity. Captain must not receive an automatically complete tactical picture that makes those exchanges unnecessary. The recommendation to automatically deliver basic new-threat information to Captain was rejected.

AI-operated Systems retain limited knowledge and communicate through the existing Coordination structure. An AI colleague's report can support coordination in a small crew; it does not justify an omniscient Captain display. This decision does not prohibit all automatic alerts or existing information transfer. Their exact scope must preserve dependence on crew communication.

### Confirmed decision — no Captain map; deliberate shared presentation

Captain has no tactical map of its own, including no map automatically assembled from crew reports. Players already have the concept of putting information **on screen** to share it with everyone. Preserve this deliberate shared presentation alongside crew conversation; do not replace it with a duplicate Captain information surface. Further Captain console detail is deferred while the interview establishes pillars and System interactions.

## Design pillars

These consolidate the agreed brief and interview answers. They are criteria for selecting gameplay, not a fixed roster or approval of every catalogue entry.

| Pillar | Design requirement | Reject when |
|---|---|---|
| Complementary expertise solves changing problems | Each player makes meaningful local decisions that affect the crew's options and outcome. Missions require overall coordination; specialist operators retain their controls. | One player decides everything, or specialist activities are isolated minigames with little shared consequence. |
| Communication is part of bridge play | Players report, interpret, request and coordinate. Information can be deliberately put on the shared Viewscreen. Limited knowledge applies to individual System AI, which communicates through Coordination. | Automatic information aggregation makes colleagues unnecessary, or talking becomes an arbitrary confirmation/transcription ritual without useful content. |
| Ongoing operation creates room for worthwhile interventions | Changing demands keep operating choices relevant. Preparation, reserves and stable settings provide enough tolerance to respond to events while ongoing demands remain. | A solved setting handles every context, repeated servicing becomes rote, or any attention diversion causes immediate collapse. |
| Individual depth, shared simulation | Beginners retain useful decisions while whole-System AI operates delegated capabilities. Experts can choose deeper work in the same session; AI and humans share physical rules, authority and knowledge limits. | Assistance turns a player into a spectator, raises colleagues' mandatory complexity, or depends on an omniscient replacement AI. |
| Dynamic situations support combat and non-combat work | Observable physical and actor causes create investigation, negotiation, rescue, logistics and layered combat choices. GM-led play remains supported and always optional. | Emergence means unexplained random crises, only combat creates meaningful activity, or a GM is needed to keep ordinary Stations occupied. |

Six players are the reference crew, with 2–8 supported through coherent dedicated human/AI Station bundles and explicit human-seeking exceptions. This is a cross-cutting constraint. Existing networking and individual per-System AI structure remain fixed. Station selection follows System/activity design rather than determining it in advance.

G001 establishes **designer-authored Station bundles per ship**: players choose a seat and its complexity, rather than designing or rearranging ownership bundles themselves. Exact roles and 7/8-player arrangements follow the activity reviews. See the [ordered review](./system-review.md#g001--station-ownership-and-authored-work-bundles) for the decision and alternatives.

G002 establishes **authored Station complexity presets only**. Selecting lower complexity must not require a complex configuration process. Players do not set arbitrary per-System depth overrides; scenarios retain the ability to require particular individual Systems under human control. Detailed preset contents remain to be designed, including the provisional reactor ladder.

G003 establishes **competent AI execution with bounded judgement**. Assistance is constrained by authored knowledge, priorities and risk limits; manual advantage comes from anticipation, interpretation, adaptable choices and coordination, rather than deliberately poor AI execution. Preserve individual per-System AI, Coordination and common physical/authority rules.

G004 establishes **ordinary Backfill for every AI-operated absence, including temporary filling-in**. It reacts to Command orders, alert state, the current mission list and the situation through its regular policies. Behaviour should generally have hysteresis, so an ongoing tow would probably continue, without a special takeover rule. No unique temporary-fill policy, intent-copying contract or reset mode is introduced.

G008 establishes **contextual activity**: meaningful ongoing loops where useful, with room for short quiet periods, preparation and listening. Every human Station still needs worthwhile decisions and influence across each scenario; filler maintenance and long exclusion remain design failures. Duty-officer away assignments are a candidate source of quieter work, queued below.

G009 establishes **prepared operating margins**, with clear warnings, rather than a generic additional grace timer. Stable reserves/readiness provide room to respond; depleted or poorly prepared state can offer less room. Exact System consequences and numerical tuning remain for their reviews.

G010 establishes **console messages for AI communication**, with urgency levels and notification sounds for higher levels. There are **no AI voices**; voice acting work is also an explicit production-cost objection. Exact levels, sound rules and accessibility remain to be designed. This preserves ordinary Coordination and human crew conversation rather than replacing them with an AI briefing.

G012 establishes **useful post-loss participation where the scenario continues**. Escape pods and duty-officer team management can let players support the remaining crew without a mothership. Joining eligible vacant Stations is also desirable when possible, but too situational to be the sole continuation route. Spectating remains optional. Pod, personnel, rescue and mission-ending mechanics await their catalogue reviews; this does not select free instant replacement ships or change networking/AI architecture.

G015 establishes **optional individual practice plus short shared exercises**, with ordinary AI filling missing roles. Teach local decisions and their consequences for colleagues, adapting exercises to crew size and Station complexity. Joining live play does not require completing a tutorial. Exact exercises follow the System and Station design.

G016 establishes **basic detected returns on relevant operational radars**, with each view suited to its Station. Sensors supplies deeper interpretation and assessment. A return does not automatically disclose identity, intentions, capabilities or a firing solution. Captain still receives reports and deliberate Viewscreen sharing, with no personal map. Detailed observation and evidence-sharing rules follow G017–G026.

G017 establishes **broad routine assessment plus optional deeper investigation**. Generally use A's progressive identification/classification/assessment for broad data that has not been obfuscated, without manually processing every contact in fleet battles. Deeper enquiries provide B's targeted detail for one-on-one and slower situations. This is observation-based automation, not guaranteed hidden truth; obfuscation and sensing limits can restrict what is learned. Exact breadth/depth allocation, scan costs and sharing remain open.

G018 establishes **confidence per finding, with concise summaries**. Identity, position and condition can have different confidence. Routine updates remain automatic, with consequential uncertainty highlighted and details available on inspection. Players do not manually maintain confidence values or inspect every contact. Observer knowledge and individual AI information limits remain intact.

G019 establishes **predicted tracks with growing positional uncertainty after observation is lost**. Prediction uses observed motion only, is clearly distinguished from a live return and retains the last actual observation for inspection. Tracking updates automatically; it does not follow unseen manoeuvres. Exact prediction horizon, expiry, reacquisition and clutter rules remain open.

G020 establishes **two independent sensor Systems: broad and focused, each drawing separate power**. Focused investigation does not consume broad sensing capacity or pause/degrade its operation directly. Both can operate concurrently; Engineering can ration their separate power demands through the shared power model. Preserve individual AI per System. Exact power requirements, controls, concurrent focus targets and intrusive-scan implementation remain for later review.

G021 establishes **operating signatures from several major Systems**, alongside explicit emitting actions. Reducing exposure can constrain useful capabilities. Use a small readable set of causes with automatic updates and stable operating priorities; exact sources, detection effects and concealment controls remain open. Human and AI observers remain subject to the same knowledge limits.

G022 establishes **selectable faction sharing of Navigation, Sensors and Tactical radar feeds**. Receiving ships decide how to use the shared information; sharing does not set their targets, routes or orders. Preserve source, age and confidence, existing knowledge limits and the Captain's lack of a personal map. Player-crewed ships have **text/audio/video Comms**. **Every ship has generic Comms requests contextual to its relationship with the caller and current objectives, separate from plot Comms.** When a human controls the receiving Comms System, that generic channel becomes text chat. AI-controlled recipients respond through ordinary dialogue/AI behaviour and limited knowledge. This does not introduce voiced AI dialogue; exact media/UI and response rules follow later reviews.

G023 establishes **automatic routine reference attachment, with an explicit unusual-record exception**. A record marked unusual is found by the player and explicitly flagged for attachment to an entity. Automatic matching and explicit attachment preserve source and uncertainty; reference information is not proof of a vessel's actual current state. Exact marking, search and attachment controls remain open.

G024 establishes **shared Comms channels for AI and human ships**, with fleet target communication and chat producing systemic traffic alongside authored transmissions. **Enemy text chat can be intercepted in team PvP.** AI communicates using its actual knowledge and objectives, not fabricated omniscient intelligence, and remains textual with no AI voices. Exact interception reach, decoding, security, disruption and audio/video interception remain open; existing networking and individual AI structure are preserved.

G024 also establishes **encryption levels and low-power public channels**, with a public chatter feed for Comms to inspect. Interception does not automatically bypass encryption. Exact levels, decoding requirements, power curves, range and feed filtering remain open.

G025 establishes **danger discovery through players' usual tools and Captain-set alert conditions**. Players observe, interpret and communicate danger; Captain can change the alert condition. This replaces the proposed automatic bridge-wide danger warning. G010's addressed console reports and urgency sounds remain; their presentation must not automatically discover danger, assemble a complete briefing or change the ship's alert condition.

G026 establishes **automatic factual mission history only**. Record supported events/reports with source and time; do not add player-authored log annotations, compulsory prose or invented reasoning. Exact visibility, recap presentation, retention and campaign persistence remain open.

G027 establishes **2D capital-ship movement, with limited up/down for ship-to-ship collision avoidance; full 3D movement for small craft such as shuttles, fighters and torpedoes**. This is a vessel/craft distinction, not a complexity setting: humans and AI use the same physical capabilities. Capital-ship vertical motion is not selected as a routine tactical dimension. Exact collision-avoidance limits and small-craft controls remain for later review.

G028 establishes **selective meaningful terrain clearing**. Some obstacles/routes should change access or exposure when cleared, with clearance preserved long enough to matter in the scenario. Avoid making every rock an objective. Exact persistence, occlusion and resource costs remain open; this does not adopt permanent galaxy-wide terrain state.

G029 establishes **hazards with contextual opportunities as well as costs**. Some regions can offer concealment, useful approaches or operating positions while impairing the crew's own sensing, Comms, movement or endurance. Effects remain coherent and shared by humans/AI; not every hazard requires an upside. Exact effects and mitigation remain open.

G030 establishes **authored field evolution plus selected causal changes from actions/events**. Observable sources and events can change the environment, supporting intervention as well as prediction. Keep the existing bounded parametric field direction; exact source types, links, time scales and forecasts remain open.

G031 establishes **static orbital bodies: planets, moons, satellites and asteroid fields**. No on-rails orbiting or orbital operation windows are selected. Objects that move do so through ordinary motion, not by orbiting a body. Retain coherent charts/reference frames and supported terrain effects; relative operations between moving ships are reviewed separately.

G032 establishes **Helm Match Speed for its selected target**. Clicking Match Speed enables persistent speed matching; touching the throttle or clicking Match Speed again cancels it. Steering remains the player's responsibility and does not cancel speed matching. This does not maintain relative position, range or aspect. Matching can become infeasible; exact target-loss/infeasibility presentation and limits remain open.

G033 establishes **bounded purposeful civilian traffic**. Selected vessels pursue scenario-relevant work such as deliveries or evacuation and respond to danger, delays and requests. Authored encounters remain available. Exact objectives, population, response and consequence rules remain open; no unbounded galaxy economy or constant traffic policing is adopted.

G034 establishes **routine traffic flow managed by local actors/services, with crew intervention for consequential exceptions and emergencies**. Traffic-focused scenarios can give the crew a larger sustained role. Exact priority, congestion, panic and emergency-authority rules remain open.

G035 establishes **degraded service capability plus critical failure/recovery thresholds**, with facilities treated like ships for damage: **shields, Systems and components**. Service loss/recovery should follow the affected machinery, rather than a separate generic facility health penalty. This selects a shared damage-model direction; exact component statuses, repair methods, parts and damage routing remain for their later reviews. Shared rules do not require identical loadouts.

G036 establishes **systemic service dependencies with automatic propagation**. Once relevant dependencies exist, failures, depleted reserves and interventions ripple through them through common rules; scenarios do not script every downstream consequence separately. Information about the ripple still depends on legitimate observation/reports. Exact dependencies, reserves and recovery rules remain open.

G037 establishes **facility workforce as teams using the same underlying system as player-ship teams**, such as repair or security teams. Do not create a separate aggregate workforce simulation or track every worker individually. Exact team roles, availability, willingness, staffing effects and shared personnel mechanics remain for later review.

G038 establishes **real stocks, shortages, deliveries and contextual exchanges without a general market-price simulation**. Payments and negotiated/authored offers can still exist. Actor work and dependencies remain systemic; exact goods, needs, consumption, production and exchange terms remain open.

G039 establishes **recognisable doctrine plus limited consistent actor variation** in priorities, risk tolerance or reserve policies, expressed through existing individual System AI. Behaviour and Comms can provide evidence of those differences. Exact profiles and discoverability remain open; no hidden stat bonuses, omniscient planner or separate personality simulation is selected.

G040 establishes **faction relationships plus individual actor trust**, based on interactions and commitments the actor knows about. Ships/organisations of one faction need not respond identically. Exact trust measures, information spread, presentation and persistence remain open; no universal morality score or omniscient reputation update is selected.

G041 establishes **content-specific formal commitments**, from authored plots or PCG side missions. Generic Comms requests do not universally create formal obligations. This replaces the proposed universal reusable commitment layer; exact procedural side-mission terms and settlement remain for mission-generation review. Ordinary requests still affect supported actor behaviour and relationships.

G042 establishes **behavioural surrender compliance**. Acceptance changes supported orders/intent rather than mechanically preventing violations or granting remote controls. Conduct can be observed, challenged or violated with coherent consequences; betrayal is not required or routine. AD56 selects whole-ship operational takeover after separate supported capture requirements are met. Exact terms, follow-up and capture/security conditions remain open.

G044 establishes **objective-driven pursuit**. Different foes pursue according to their own objectives: a base defender may break off early, while an actor hunting the players can continue across travel when its knowledge and capabilities allow. Do not impose one universal chase/leash rule. Exact tracking, break-off criteria and travel representation remain open within the existing individual AI structure.

G045 establishes **selective route-changing anomalies alongside investigation, resources and local effects**. Some phenomena can change usable connections, such as a wormhole route, where that creates worthwhile crew choices. Exact types, transit rules, costs, discovery and persistence remain open; no universal portal implementation or instant-kill black-hole rule is selected.

G046 establishes **salvage of actual surviving cargo and useful components, alongside materials where appropriate**. Recovery choices reflect what remains and what the crew needs. Exact compatibility, condition, extraction/recycling, part conversion and inventory representation remain open; not every component must become a separate inventory item.

G047 establishes **the coarse wrapping planetary map mode**, with normal planar capital-ship controls and static planets. This does not restore orbital body motion. **Dropfleet Commander** is an additional research reference for planetary/orbital activities; [initial primary-source notes](./research/dropfleet-commander.md) distinguish verified scope from proposed transfers. Exact sites, deployment, transit and mode-transition rules remain open.

G048 establishes **a primary offensive target with separate assignments/priorities for supported individual weapon Systems**. Simple presets retain main-target play; ordinary System AI executes delegated engagements. Science designation remains advisory. Exact assignment authority, selection policies and acquisition limits remain open; this is not emitter-by-emitter administration or a networking change.

G049 establishes **graduated, weapon-dependent effectiveness within the firing envelope**. Distance, relative motion or supported firing-solution quality can materially improve or weaken an attack where appropriate to the weapon, creating positioning and exposure choices. Ordinary System AI handles routine aiming/execution. Exact falloff, tracking, guidance and information dependencies remain open; no universal accuracy roll or compulsory manual aiming is adopted.

G050 establishes **optional, equipment-dependent shield probing**. With suitable scanners, a crew can dedicate time to finding shield weaknesses; foes can do the same. The advantage is small against most foes, but particular shields or penetrating phasers can make the interaction much more consequential. Findings generally persist for the observed configuration without continuous probing. Special enemy equipment may rotate shield or phaser frequencies, making fresh assessment useful. Detection of those changes, rotation timing and exact weapon/shield interactions remain open; the previously suggested universal modulation trade-off and switching costs are not adopted.

G051 establishes **interception that can protect other assets within actual coverage**. Supported incoming threats to allies or civilians can be engaged when detection, range and geometry permit. Limited capacity makes protection priorities compete with the firing ship's own safety; ordinary System AI handles routine tracking/interception. Exact equipment, capacity, costs and authority remain open. This does not mandate a missile-first engagement sequence or a separate defence Station.

G052 establishes **broadsides as an equipment-dependent combat style**. Suitable hulls can gain important opportunities from coordinated broadside passes, while other designs favour different positions and tactics. Ordinary weapon-System AI handles routine gunnery; Helm owns the manoeuvre. Normal cooling spends finite stock. **S034 adopts optional fitted recycling at a loss**, extending endurance without eliminating net consumption. Exact collection, recovery yield, throughput, power demand and stock handling remain open. The previously queued reactor complexity ladder also remains provisional.

**Coolant expenditure follows actual heat removed.** Allocated cooling limits available heat-removal rate; a cool System does not spend coolant merely because capacity is allocated. Reserved capacity remains unavailable elsewhere until reallocated. Active cooling still consumes finite stock and optional recycling remains lossy. Exact rates, throughput and operating costs remain open.

Exact thermal failure and recovery behaviour remains for the relevant System reviews.

G057 establishes **component conditions as the primary damage model, replacing System percentage damage**. Exact part categories, recipes/quantities, substitution, test controls, reporting, travel and repair-versus-replacement rules remain open. G059 establishes **damage-condition-specific escalation and spread**. Some debuffs can worsen or spread through supported causal rules; others remain stable and are safe to leave untreated. Supported isolation/containment can limit relevant escalation, with consequences following systemic dependencies rather than scenario scripts. Exact conditions, paths, warnings, intervention margins and containment controls remain open; no universal contagion or arbitrary cascade cap is adopted.

G060 establishes **stress-dependent wear**, caused by hard use, overheating or particular adverse conditions. Healthy ordinary operation does not inevitably create a recurring maintenance timer. Crews can influence wear through operating choices and delegate routine maintenance execution to the relevant System AI. Exact stress sources, thresholds, rates, maintenance methods and parts requirements remain open.

G061 establishes **operation-dependent withdrawal commitments, principally recovery of deployed crew/equipment**. External work that sends resources out should normally plan to recover them; breaking off can leave them needing recovery. A tractor beam deploys neither crew nor equipment and is safe to release in this respect. Exact recovery methods and consequences remain for personnel/craft/equipment reviews; no universal interruption penalty or loss of completed work is adopted.

G062 retains **asymmetric hulls and doctrines under already agreed constraints**: equipment-dependent combat styles (G052), particular shield/weapon interactions (G050), objective-dependent pursuit (G044) and recognisable doctrine with limited actor variation (G039). ## Selected ship System directions

S001 establishes **variable-duration sustained beam attacks**, able to continue or stop within geometry, local battery and thermal limits. Ordinary individual System AI handles delegated aiming/firing and conservation; operators choose targets, timing and policy. Exact bank variants, output, interruption and recovery limits remain open. This is a proposal change from the Current authored active-period/cooldown model.

S002 establishes **energy-bolt and ammunition-fed kinetic projectile banks**, sharing lead/volley concepts while retaining different resource constraints. Kinetic banks consume physical ammunition as well as operating power. Equipment and roles are authored rather than required on every hull; ordinary AI handles routine lead/firing. Exact variants, resource costs and charge/volley rules remain open.

S003 establishes **link-dependent post-launch instructions for suitable guided ordnance**, alongside fixed-intent/independent variants. Preserve physical payload properties at launch and ordinary AI execution. **For the torpedo/PDC/broadside combat model, torpedoes should be much longer-ranged and slower than Current tuning**, leaving meaningful travel, detection, interception and adaptation time. This remains a combat design reference rather than a required sequence for every ship. Exact speed, endurance/range, commands and link-loss behaviour remain open.

S004 establishes **shared loading/handling capacity within authored launcher groups where fitted**, with priorities deciding which tubes reload first. Independent-loader designs remain possible. Ordinary AI handles routine scheduling and target readiness from actual stocks; no round-by-round loading chore or compulsory single ship-wide loader is added. Exact grouping, throughput, damage and payload-change behaviour remain open.

S005 retains **optional weapon tuning to exploit investigated shield weaknesses under G050**: suitable equipment, usually modest benefit, stronger interactions for particular shields/penetrating weapons, and generally persistent findings unless configuration changes. Ordinary AI can handle delegated tuning. No universal frequency-guessing ritual or mandatory recurring retune is added; exact calibration controls remain open.

S006 establishes **weapon energy reserves using local System batteries**, without a separate shot-energy capacitor layer. Equipment can still have timed preparation, staged actions and particular discharge/recovery rules. Engineering retains flow allocation and ordinary individual System AI handles delegated readiness/firing. Exact charge timing, cancellation and thermal behaviour remain open.

S007 establishes **distinct side-on geometry for broadside batteries**, making orientation and relative course consequential. **Turret variants can have better arcs with finite tracking speed**, so wider coverage does not mean instant alignment or unlimited ability to follow a close fast target. Ordinary AI handles aiming while Helm owns movement. Exact arcs, tracking rates, battery grouping and resource/range profiles remain open.

S008 establishes **dual-use PDCs where effective**, permitting short-range attacks on suitable ships/craft as well as interception. Both uses compete for actual tracking, ammunition, power and thermal capacity. Ordinary AI handles aiming/interception under priority and reserve policies; exact effectiveness and rules remain open.

S009 establishes **physical recoil for selected heavy guns**, as a reusable authored weapon behaviour. Impulse can make mounting and simultaneous/staggered firing matter, with ordinary assistance handling routine compensation where supported. Heavy long-range kinetic equipment uses the common weapon model; exact impulse, handling, range and resource profiles remain open.

S010 establishes **component EMP debuffs that refresh on reapplication, paired with a longer-duration stacking EMP-resistance buff**. Resistance reduces the duration of subsequent applications; **reapplication keeps the greater of the current remaining time and the new resistance-adjusted duration**. Temporary disruption recovers without repair when its debuff expires. These are reusable damage/status behaviours independent of projectile form. Exact durations, reduction formula, stack decay/caps and application ordering remain open.

S011 establishes **physical collateral from area effects**: physically exposed eligible entities can be affected regardless of faction or relationship. Target-selection and risk policies constrain firing rather than grant allies or civilians immunity. Ordinary System AI uses available evidence to avoid known unsafe opportunities under its configured rules. Exact area shape, falloff, cover, detonation and safety controls remain open.

S012 establishes **physical marking beacons delivered by suitable payloads**. They provide limited observations while operating and communicating, rather than an abstract targeting bonus or permanent perfect track. Routine delivery/reporting can be delegated to ordinary System AI. Exact measurements, lifetime, communication, removal and countermeasures remain open. Equipment-specific penetration remains a retained payload candidate; exact shield/armour routing is unresolved.

S013 establishes **equipment-dependent mine trigger discrimination**. Suitable mines use authored trigger policies and locally available sensing/identification; simple indiscriminate proximity variants remain possible. Recognition does not use hidden faction truth, and selective triggering does not protect nearby allies from physical collateral after detonation. Exact deployment, trigger controls, recognition, expiry/recovery and countermeasures remain open.

S014 establishes **limited shared material inputs between ammunition fabrication and repair supply/production**. Authored recipes can overlap selected material categories while retaining specialised inputs and finished parts. Ordinary AI maintains desired stocks within configured reserve policies; shared inputs do not make every finished part universally interchangeable. Exact recipes, input categories, reserve controls and throughput remain open.

S015 establishes **prepared fire groups from authored or saved configurations**, with **individual weapon-System overrides during play**. An operator can de-group a weapon for an individual assignment when needed; this exception does not introduce general live group construction or barrel-pattern editing. Group commands must not overwrite the active individual override. Each System retains ordinary AI, knowledge, readiness and authority. Exact override cancellation/rejoining and coordinated-release controls remain open.

S016 establishes **physical damage paths through the same authored ship map used for repair**, refined by AD39–50. Incoming damage selects a weighted random deck independently of shot Z height, then follows a 2D path through its compartments. Materials and structural HP belong to compartments; resistance is charged by path length, and compartment damage applies component status effects. Attackers scan to identify the compartment containing equipment and receive firing-angle advice from available evidence. Components have no precise internal positions; faults are selected from the compartment-wide eligible pool under AD48. A frontal attack toward engines still encounters intervening compartments, and target selection does not guarantee the engine's deck. Progressive breakthrough uses compartment damage rather than individual wall/part HP or a local hole grid; AD46 makes resistance fall progressively as compartment HP falls. Ordinary weapon AI keeps the same knowledge limits. AD47 selects damage-event rules using deposited damage amount/type and component susceptibility to determine eligible effects, chance and severity; small hits need not cause a fault. Exact deck weights, payload deposition, effect weights, structural-repair work rules and angle-advice precision remain open. S025 already permits full field structural restoration where resources, teams, access and time allow. AD49 adds a disabling condition to the contents at zero compartment HP; restoring the compartment clears that condition while unrelated component faults remain. AD50 selects an authored recovery threshold above zero and below full HP; after recovery, disablement reapplies only at zero HP.

S017 establishes **sortie management for carried fighters and attack drones**: players assign bounded missions, priorities and recall orders while ordinary individual craft System AI handles flight and execution. Preparation, reconnaissance, protection, attack and recovery supply bridge decisions without direct human piloting in this redesign's initial scope. Full-3D small-craft movement remains selected. Exact craft families, endurance, command links, launch/recovery capacity and losses remain open.

S018 establishes **signal- and geometry-dependent jamming**, including possible interference with friendly functions. A targeted jammer can track its selected contact while still affecting other craft within its actual coverage. Reuse suitable arc, range, turret and traversal behaviours from the weapon model for jammers, tractor beams and other aimed equipment, without making their effects or resources identical.

**Complex interactions must be surfaced through relevant player tools and ordinary communication.** For example, a contact made unreachable by the ship's own jamming can appear unavailable in Comms with an explanation that it is being jammed. An affected ship that can still communicate should report the interference to the attacker, including friendly-fire cases, through the normal channels and its available evidence. Neither feedback nor reports grant hidden knowledge or bypass a blocked link; uncertainty must remain explicit where the cause is not established. Exact coverage, interference strength, attribution and reporting controls remain open.

S019 establishes **onboard spoof emitters plus deployed equipment represented as ordinary ship entities**. Player and AI ships use the same model and rules; only the controllers of some Systems differ. A deployed turret is a ship with no engines, a weapon, a battery and simple ordinary AI. A deployed decoy is a short-lived ship with no engines, a battery and a sensor-spoofing System. Other deployables follow the same composition principle rather than bespoke entity rules. Decoy effectiveness follows the observing sensor or guidance equipment's evidence, rather than guaranteeing diversion or giving an apparent spoof contact the capabilities of a real ship. Exact profiles, deployment, lifetime, recovery and countermeasures remain open.

S020 establishes **finite, prioritised electronic protection selected at System level, or Station level in simplified presets**. Do not require component-by-component protection allocation. Ordinary individual System AI handles routine execution within the selected priorities; a simplified Station choice maps to the supported Systems without changing their physical protection rules. Protected/degraded status must be clear. Exact capacity, costs, supported Systems and allocation policy remain open.

S021 establishes **bounded remote access and disruption without hostile System takeover**. Suitable authored operations can obtain supported information, open access paths or interfere with capabilities, but do not let an attacker command an enemy System. Ordinary individual System AI executes delegated operations using normal knowledge and communication. These are in-fiction effects; existing networking and command admission remain unchanged. Exact prerequisites, delivery, effects, duration and countermeasures remain open.

S022 establishes **directional shield strength separate from generator local batteries**. Incoming hits deplete the affected arc's protection. Raised shields consume battery energy continuously, with most shield-related consumption coming from recharging protection. This operating draw is distinct from the already selected fullness-dependent passive battery loss. Engineering supplies flow; Shields manages protection and communicates recovery needs to Helm. Exact upkeep/recharge rates, generator-to-arc topology and collapse behaviour remain open.

S023 establishes **battery-mediated shield redistribution**. An operator can discharge protection from a donor arc into a battery, then use that reserve to recharge another arc faster than relying only on incoming reactor flow. Donor protection falls, and receiving protection still follows its recharge process rather than an instantaneous direct arc transfer. Ordinary Shields System AI can execute delegated priorities. Existing battery/thermal rules apply; exact conversion efficiency, discharge/recharge rates, capacity handling and battery-to-arc topology remain open.

S024 establishes **defensive retuning as a consistent capability of every shield generator**, with equipment differences in how well it performs. **Retune while raised through a timed transition that temporarily reduces protection and consumes battery energy/generates heat.** Ordinary System AI can operate delegated retuning using available evidence. This supersedes the equipment-only availability recommendation, while retaining optional attacker probing and generally persistent findings until configuration changes. Exact duration, reduction, costs, performance differences, interruption and change detection remain open; universal availability does not require constant manual frequency matching.

S025 establishes **resource-dependent full field restoration of armour and structure**. Suitable parts/materials, teams, access and time can restore original protection using the shared repair model. Large or catastrophic damage can still exceed field capability and require facilities under G064. Restoration is not instant and does not impose a universal weaker-patch ceiling. AD49 makes structural restoration remove the condition disabling the contents of a zero-HP compartment; individual component faults still need their own remedies. ### Composable weapon model — selected direction

Use a **generic configurable weapon System with a library of reusable behaviours**. **Firing patterns are authored modes, selected rather than constructed during play.** A weapon lists its barrel markers; an ordered firing cycle of arbitrary authored length specifies one or more of those markers at each step. ## Candidate System interactions — power, reserves and cooling

### Confirmed — sustainable output and continuous support

An undamaged ship cannot sustain every System at full output indefinitely. Ordinary cruise can settle into a forgiving stable state, while high-output operations compete for capacity. Reserves permit bursts and give time to respond to crises.

The power operator listens to what other players are using and continuously adjusts controls to support their work, including outside crises. A stable ship still offers changing support choices; it does not reduce the operator to waiting for an alarm. In high-power situations, the operator must ration capacity between competing uses. This is ongoing communication through listening and adaptation, with deliberate requests or negotiation when scarcity requires it. No routine request/approval handshake is introduced by this decision.

Exact System ownership within the eventual Station roster, resource layers, allocation controls and consumption rules remain open. Individual System AI and the existing networking remain fixed constraints.

### Confirmed — flow allocation and local batteries

Power controls determine the **flow rate into each System**, rather than directly setting its output ceiling. Systems have local batteries and consume from those batteries. Increasing flow in advance builds a reserve for upcoming work; the local reserve separates immediate operation from the current supply allocation.

Each local battery has a passive drain that increases with how full it is. Maintaining a high/full reserve is therefore inefficient. The operator listens for upcoming and current activity, prepares the relevant reserves, then adjusts flows rather than keeping every battery permanently full. Under high demand, rationed flow affects how long colleagues can sustain work and how quickly they can restore readiness. S038 selects energy-limited operation at exhaustion without a generic restart lock. **Intake is headroom-limited:** actual accepted flow falls to what consumption, passive loss and available capacity permit; unaccepted energy is not drawn from the source. This does not automatically reallocate reserved flow to other Systems. The precise drain curve and equipment-specific recovery remain open.

Heat accumulates from changes in local battery level, both increases and decreases. Systems have modifiers determining how hot they run. This establishes local storage and bidirectional thermal costs as the selected **proposal model**, not as claims about current runtime behaviour. Cooling controls, exact heat arithmetic and the possible role of a separate ship-level reserve still need decisions.

### Confirmed — simultaneous charging and consumption

**Option B is agreed:** charging and depletion generate heat separately, even when they occur simultaneously at equal rates and the displayed battery level stays constant. Each System's thermal modifier applies. Exact coefficients, passive-drain treatment and conversion losses remain unresolved for the later power/thermal review.

#### Research comparison

- **EmptyEpsilon:** power allocation changes System performance; overpowering increases heat and energy draw, while Engineering distributes a limited simultaneous coolant allowance. This provides a reference for coupled output/thermal choices, rather than local-battery preparation. [Official Engineering guide](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3).
- **Elite Dangerous:** its official Horizons manual describes allocation priorities filling SYS/ENG/WEP capacitors at different rates. Actions requiring capacitor energy cannot activate without sufficient charge, even if the module has operating power. Allocation also has direct performance effects. This is a useful recharge-versus-reserve reference, but uses three grouped capacitors rather than a battery per System. [Frontier manual, Power Distributor, printed pp. 64–65](https://d1wv0x2frmpnh.cloudfront.net/elite/website/assets/English-PlayersGuide_v2.00-Horizons.pdf). Historical manual verified on 2026-10-02; no current-version balance values are assumed.

Phoenix's increasing drain at high charge is its own agreed proposal rule. Neither reference above establishes that rule or the proposed heat formula. The comparisons help distinguish preparing reserves, sustaining performance and allocating cooling; they do not require copying either game's roster or exact controls.

#### Alternatives considered

| Option | At equal inflow and consumption | Player consequence — design inference |
|---|---|---|
| A — Heat from net battery-level movement | No change-derived heat while the level is steady. | Rewards precisely matching flow to demand, but sustained heavy operation could run cool once balanced. Decide whether that is the intended reward rather than assuming it is a bug. |
| B — Heat from charging and depletion counted separately | Both movements generate heat even though they cancel in the displayed level. | Sustained work needs cooling; reserves buy time and charging before/after an operation also has a thermal cost. One consistent rule can apply across Systems with thermal modifiers. |
| C — Charging heat plus activity-specific heat | Charging generates heat; the active task supplies its own thermal cost rather than a universal depletion term. | Allows lasers, engines, sensors and other Systems to have distinct operating heat rules, but adds exceptions to teach and tune. This is an alternative to, not an adopted interpretation of, the user's bidirectional rule. |

**Selected: B.** Sustained demand remains thermally relevant and preparing/restoring reserves has a thermal cost. This is a Phoenix proposal decision, not a realism claim or a mechanic verified in either reference. No exact formula is adopted yet.

### Ongoing activities

The user's passive activities are persistent operating demands, rather than inactivity. A player maintains a useful state while listening to the bridge and adjusting to changing needs. Power, reserve energy and cooling are an example: the interesting question is which balance serves the current plan and preserves future options.

A stable configuration should stay viable long enough to allow a short diversion of attention. Demand, damage, environment and other officers' requests can change the preferred configuration, giving the operator reasons to reconsider it. If one configuration solves every situation, it is not a worthwhile dedicated job. If the player must constantly reset it regardless of circumstances, it is a chore.

Listening is part of this loop. “We are about to accelerate” can prompt Engineering to prepare; “the repair hold will last longer” can change defence or reserve policy. Such communication can be continuous and informal without requiring a request-confirmation ritual for every small adjustment.

### Event-driven activities

The user's active activities are interventions caused by something happening: an uncertain return, a threatened civilian, a changing claim, a disabled engine, a tow opportunity or an incoming salvo. A player identifies an option, coordinates what it needs and commits an action with an observable consequence.

Every human Station needs a meaningful share of these opportunities in each scenario. A share means useful decisions and effects, not an identical count of button presses. The scenario may seed events, but physical or social systems should produce follow-on problems where practical. Quiet periods should support preparation, interpretation or deliberate recovery rather than compulsory filler.

Active communication requests scarce capacity or coordinates an opportunity: target designation, thrust margin, a firing window, a safe approach or permission to transfer. The interaction earns its place when the recipient makes a real choice or adjusts a plan. Automatic routine sharing can coexist with deliberate interpretation and negotiation.

### Attention and flow

Flow is a working design hypothesis: challenge, feedback and control should fit the operator's current skill. It is not a promised psychological outcome. Good play can include tension, reflection, uncertainty and brief rest as well as sustained concentration.

Assess each activity along several dimensions: monitoring frequency, number of coupled variables, urgency, consequences of delay, uncertainty, manual execution and communication demand. “Complexity” cannot be reduced to how many Systems a Station owns. A long dialogue at a deadline can demand more attention than several automated weapon banks.

Margins can come from batteries, cool machinery, prepared tubes, spare teams, route clearance or actor patience. They should be visible, buildable and spendable. A crisis can consume them without instantly making every ongoing job fail. Degraded operation and recoverable loss should leave choices after an imperfect response.

## Whole-System delegation

The current fine-System/control-source machinery is a useful foundation. Retain its single admitted command path and common physical constraints; the proposal concerns what the player chooses to operate and what the assistant needs to understand.

The beginner's targeting System can remain human while separate phaser Systems automatically execute fire against that selection. Human steering can coexist with an AI tractor System engaging an approved mission target when range, geometry and eligibility permit. “Approved” matters: auto-tractor should not grab every nearby craft, and auto-fire should not decide to attack a protected civilian merely because it is reachable.

| Delegation question | Proposal requirement |
|---|---|
| What is delegated? | A named complete System with a coherent task, not an undisclosed collection of interventions. |
| Who chooses intent? | The operator supplies a target, posture, priorities or bounded mission task; authored Backfill supplies a viable default when the seat is AI-controlled. |
| What can it decide? | Routine execution within that intent, including starting/stopping when ordinary prerequisites change. |
| What remains deliberate? | Risk limits, target/beneficiary choices and protected promises/escalation unless the crew or scenario explicitly authorises delegation. Human-seeking exceptions are available. |
| How is depth selected? | Designer-authored per-Station presets, without independent per-System depth selection. Presets select whole-System delegation; scenarios can require specified Systems under human control. Ordinary handoff remains supported. |
| What does the player see? | Current owner, intent, progress, resource use and a reason when blocked or deviating. |
| How does control return? | An explicit transfer with retained context, no competing actuator writers and no sudden stale input. Exact UI remains to be designed. |
| Why stay manual? | Greater situational judgement, adaptable priorities or chosen execution challenge. Avoid arbitrary AI incompetence that makes assistance a trap. |

The proposal must test the heavily assisted beginner's remaining activity. Leaving only a target button is useful only if choosing targets remains interesting and feedback is clear. Equally, a whole AI Station must keep operating on a two-player ship without forcing the remaining players to supervise every action.

## Dynamic systems and emergent work

Use a shared chain of causes: an actor or physical dependency changes; an observation becomes available; a Station interprets it; the crew selects an intervention; the intervention changes the same state. Sensors and Comms should find practical information, rather than be handed disconnected clues while another script secretly determines success.

For example, a **hypothetical** damaged transfer hub could lose throughput while civilian arrivals continue. Sensors measures the fault and remaining capacity; Comms discovers urgency and permission; Navigation selects a holding route; Helm establishes a working position; Engineering reallocates resources or repairs; Tactical/defence protects the exposed operation if threats exist. The crew might tow a stranded craft, negotiate a delay, transfer stock or reroute arrivals. This is an activity example, not a change to Falling Skyway or an adopted new scenario.

Emergent work needs bounded consequences and discoverable causes. A failure cascade should create several choices, not instantly collapse all systems. Randomising the next emergency is not sufficient: repeated contexts should change which response is good, and different interventions should change later conditions. Generation and GM support can help author/rehearse this, but the ordinary game should be playable without a facilitator.

| Dependencies | Who can help, who is affected, and what scarce resource or permission is needed? |
| Communication | What can be overheard/shared automatically, and what needs deliberate coordination? |
| Margin | What preparation buys time, what consumes it, and what happens if response is delayed? |
| Delegation | Which whole System can AI own, with what intent and retained human decision? |
| Depth | What can a novice meaningfully retain? What extra judgement can an expert choose? |
| Failure and recovery | Can an imperfect attempt leave interesting options rather than total loss or a reset chore? |
| Quiet and busy periods | What does the operator do before, during and after the event? |
| Evidence | What observation would show this loop is engaging, dull, overloaded or dominated? |

## Evaluation and safeguards

| Failure to guard against | Design approach to investigate | Playtest evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Overload | Coherent bundles, visible reserves, meaningful delegation and bounded simultaneous deadlines. | Missed changes, frantic tab switching, abandoned goals and whether assistants restore agency. |
| Waiting | Parallel preparation/investigation, useful continuing work and recovery options after loss. | Unoccupied periods and the player's account of whether they were choosing, watching intentionally or excluded. |
| Rote chores | Changing constraints, presets/assistants for solved execution and actions with alternatives. | Repeated identical input sequences with little changed reasoning. |
| One player directing everyone | Local information and decision rights, requests with genuine trade-offs, no universal control dashboard. | Who proposes plans, chooses methods, declines requests and determines consequential outcomes. |
| Sensors/Comms event starvation | Observable moving actors, dependencies and competing claims rather than scripted dialogue alone. | New useful findings and how often another player changes a plan because of them. |
| Combat only engages a subset | Geometry, defence, endurance, contact quality and external objectives that interact. | Decisions and effects per Station across an engagement, not simply weapon firing count. |
| Assisted play becomes spectator play | Keep a useful target/route/priority decision and make AI effects legible. | Novice can explain what they chose and why it mattered; can change depth without disrupting colleagues. |
| Depth becomes mandatory | Personal depth plus viable AI execution; real-time essentials available without digging through detail screens. | Mixed-skill crew can succeed without the expert operating every seat. |
| Emergence becomes unfair chaos | Observable causes, limited cascades, recovery paths and multiple viable interventions. | Players can explain a failure, identify an alternative and attempt recovery. |

Use representative travel/observation, negotiation, external work, combat and recovery situations. Start with six humans at mixed depth, then test two humans with AI Stations and eight humans with deliberate dedicated bundles. Scenarios must be assessed end to end so a Station's one exciting moment does not hide a long inactive period.

Record interventions, reasoning, resource requests, AI handoffs, attention conflicts, waiting and irreversible choices. Pair logs with observation and player reports; activity counts and win rates cannot establish flow. Quantitative time/decision targets should be set after an initial activity prototype, not invented as facts here.
